Faithful · a GenLayer intelligent contract

A translation is certified only when it carries the same commitments

Three axes, judged separately, because they fail differently. The model reports what it finds; it is never asked whether the translation passes. That decision is made in code, with thresholds every validator applies identically.

Fidelity · blocks

Do the numbers, dates, names, obligations and negations survive? A translation that reads beautifully but moves a number is not faithful.

Coverage · blocks

How much of the source is there at all. Missing sentences and untranslated blocks land here, not on fidelity.

Fluency · never blocks

Does it read like the language, or like a machine. A clumsy but correct translation is certified with reservations — refusing it would send the translator back to fix something that was not broken. Conflating this with fidelity is how a fluent, wrong translation gets published.

The thresholds live in the contract, not in the prompt. A model that is asked to score cannot approve anything on its own, because approving is not something it is asked to do. Validators must agree on the verdict each derives independently — and below a floor the scores are not compared at all, since two readers who both saw it fall short have already agreed about everything the contract acts on.
