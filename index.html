A translation is certified only when it carries the same commitments

New here? What this is, in four lines

Pick your community. The site hands you a short English passage about that community's own history. You translate it and submit both texts together.

Five validators then read the pair independently and each scores three things: whether the numbers, dates and obligations survived, how much of the source is actually there, and whether it reads like a person wrote it.

The model is never asked whether your translation passes. It reports what it found; the thresholds live in the contract, where every validator applies the same ones. A lenient reader cannot wave anything through on its own.

Fidelity and coverage can refuse a translation. Fluency never does — a faithful translation that reads clumsily is certified with reservations, because refusing it would send you back to fix something that was not broken.

1

Connect, and get a register

A register is one deployed Faithful contract — it holds the certificates. You need one before anything can be certified, so this step comes first. Deploy your own, or paste an address somebody has shared.

Checked with a free read before anything is signed: an address that does not answer rules() is refused here rather than costing you a signature to find out.

2

Choose your community and take a passage

Ten passages for each community, about its own history, and the button does not repeat one until you have seen them all. They are short on purpose — a passage you can translate in two minutes gets translated.

3

Translate it

Both boxes carry dir="auto", so the browser takes writing direction from the text itself rather than from a list of languages somebody remembered to update.
